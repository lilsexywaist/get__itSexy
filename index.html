Marta Kjorésica

Marta Kjorésica Montemayor, known simply as Sica, was born into old money, raised in a grand estate in La Moraleja, Madrid, where antique clocks echoed through halls filled with priceless art, vintage cars, and generations of family history. By twelve, she spoke four languages, spent her afternoons in etiquette lessons, weekends yachting, summers in Mallorca, and winters in Gstaad.


She preferred cigarette smoke over ballroom perfume, techno over orchestras, and nights that lasted until sunrise. While her family expected another polished socialite, Sica chased experiences instead. She knew the best clubs in Ibiza, hidden jazz bars in Paris, and cafés in Milan where midnight still felt like morning.


When her family settled in Forbes Park, Makati, society expected her to host charity luncheons. Instead, she became a familiar face in Manila's nightlife.Arriving in couture, dancing barefoot before dawn, and leaving with memories instead of headlines.


People often mistook her carefree nature for entitlement, but money had never been what drove her. To Sica, it simply made freedom easier. Behind her effortless smile was the quiet weight of the Montemayor name and the expectations that came with it. She ignored them not out of rebellion, but because she believed life should be lived, not carefully curated.


"La buena vida no se hereda. Se vive." The good life isn't inherited. It's lived.


Because designer heels wear out, champagne goes flat, and private jets always land. But the nights worth remembering almost always begin with someone saying—


"Una copa más."
